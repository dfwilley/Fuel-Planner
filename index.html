Planning assumptions

Climb, descent, and taxi fuel are planning assumptions, not a performance chart. Descent is flown at the cruise KTAS above.

Not possible with these inputs
Each leg is climb, then cruise, then descent, in still air. Climb time is (top of climb − departure elevation) ÷ climb rate, and climb distance is that time at climb speed. Descent time is (top of climb − arrival elevation) ÷ descent rate, flown at cruise KTAS. Cruise distance is the leg distance minus climb and descent distance, at cruise KTAS and the cruise fuel burn. If climb plus descent would be longer than the leg, the top of climb is lowered so those two segments exactly equal the distance and there is no cruise. Leg fuel = taxi/takeoff + climb fuel + cruise fuel + descent fuel. Outbound is from the starting airport to the candidate and the return is the reverse, so the legs differ when the field elevations differ. Distance and bearing are the great-circle track between airport reference points. An identifier that is not in the Tucson-area list is looked up in a bundled directory of US airports (OurAirports, public domain). If it is not there either, enter latitude, longitude, and field elevation. Outbound is legal if fuel on board − outbound fuel ≥ reserve (30 or 45 min at the cruise fuel burn). Gallons bought = ending fuel + outbound fuel + return fuel − fuel on board (tank max 50). Flight time is both legs' climb, cruise, and descent, plus 3 min of taxi per leg. Airports farther than the maximum distance are left out. Field elevations are FAA airport data via aviationweather.gov (meters, converted to feet) or, where that feed has no record, the AirNav surveyed field elevation. Prices are a snapshot from AirNav, SkyVector, GlobalAir and FltPlan and don't update by themselves. San Carlos Apache (P13) left out: no dated price. The pilot in command checks weather, NOTAMs and TFRs and makes the go/no-go call.
