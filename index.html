Planning assumptions

Climb, descent, and taxi fuel are planning assumptions, not a performance chart. Descent is flown at the cruise KTAS above.

Effective $/gal delivered = fuel cost ÷ gallons gained on arrival. Gallons gained = fuel landing at the destination − (fuel on board now − fuel burned flying direct from the start to the destination). When both airports are the same, that direct burn is zero and the trip is a round trip back to the start.

Each leg is climb, then cruise, then descent, in still air. Climb time is (top of climb − departure elevation) ÷ climb rate, and climb distance is that time at climb speed. Descent time is (top of climb − arrival elevation) ÷ descent rate, flown at cruise KTAS. Cruise distance is the leg distance minus climb and descent distance, at cruise KTAS and the cruise fuel burn. If climb plus descent would be longer than the leg, the top of climb is lowered so those two segments exactly equal the distance and there is no cruise. Leg fuel = taxi/takeoff + climb fuel + cruise fuel + descent fuel. A fuel stop is two legs, start to the stop and then the stop to the destination. Each leg has its own distance, true course, and top of climb. When the destination is the start, the second leg is the flight home and the plan is a round trip. Distance and bearing are the great-circle track between airport reference points. An identifier that is not in the Tucson-area list is looked up in a bundled directory of US airports (OurAirports, public domain). If it is not there either, enter latitude, longitude, and field elevation. A leg is legal when fuel at departure minus fuel burned is at least the reserve (30 or 45 min at the cruise fuel burn). The first leg leaves on the fuel already on board. At the stop you buy enough to land at the destination with the fuel you asked for, and you cannot leave with more than 50 gallons. Gallons bought = landing fuel + both legs' fuel − fuel on board. The destination is also listed when it has a fuel price: fly there on the fuel already aboard and buy on arrival. Stops farther than the maximum distance from both the start and the destination are left out. Effective $/gal delivered = fuel cost ÷ gallons gained at the destination. Gallons gained = landing fuel − (fuel on board − the direct start-to-destination burn). When the start and destination are the same, that burn is zero. Flight time is both legs' climb, cruise, and descent, plus 3 min of taxi per leg. Field elevations are FAA airport data via aviationweather.gov (meters, converted to feet) or, where that feed has no record, the AirNav surveyed field elevation. Prices are a snapshot from AirNav, SkyVector, GlobalAir and FltPlan and don't update by themselves. San Carlos Apache (P13) left out: no dated price. The pilot in command checks weather, NOTAMs and TFRs and makes the go/no-go call.
